Featured case study · URA Dream Lab

Delivering a nationally visible experience when the margin for error was zero.

A 27-day project. A fixed launch date. A senior government audience at the opening ceremony. And usability testing that revealed the core experience was broken.

Urban Redevelopment Authority

In brief

URA wanted citizens to actively shape Singapore's urban future rather than respond to it. Trinax co-engineered a domain-informed AI visualisation system with URA's planning team, caught four launch-critical failures through compressed usability testing, and shipped on schedule for the SG60 opening ceremony. The exhibition then scaled to 14 locations nationwide, generating 20,645 public ideas in four months, a 10-20x acceleration over traditional consultation.

01 · The challenge

When public engagement had to move from consultation to co-creation.

Public engagement in urban planning has historically been complex, technical, and difficult for the public to participate in meaningfully. Traditional formats, physical exhibitions, written surveys, focus groups, town halls, capture opinions but rarely stimulate creativity or dialogue.

For Draft Master Plan 2025, the Urban Redevelopment Authority wanted to fundamentally rethink this model: could citizens actively shape Singapore's urban future rather than just respond to it?

/Low accessibility · urban planning concepts are difficult for the public to visualise
/Limited engagement depth · traditional formats capture opinions but not creativity
/Slow feedback cycles · public input takes months to translate into usable insights

The exhibition launched at SG60, with a senior government audience attending the opening ceremony. The tolerance for failure was effectively zero.

02 · The approach

Domain-informed AI, not off-the-shelf generative AI.

The most distinctive innovation was not that Dream Lab used generative AI. It was how the AI was built. Trinax co-engineered the visualisation system with URA's planning team, ensuring every AI-generated concept reflected Singapore's actual urban planning principles, real-world constraints, local environmental context, and architectural expertise.

The result was an AI system that produced visuals that were imaginative yet credible, aspirational yet grounded, futuristic yet authentic to Singapore.

/AI engine co-engineered between Trinax and URA's planning team
/Domain-informed generation grounded in Singapore's urban design principles · Gemini, Flux and Luma models
/Built with cybersecurity standards aligned with ISO 27001 and DPTM

03 · The process

Built through rigour, not assumptions.

Compressed usability testing surfaced what the production team could not see. Loading states gave no feedback. Key buttons were undiscoverable. The exhibition did not work on iPhone. Most critically, the co-create mode was silently discarding all contributions except the last two, undermining the entire collective creation premise.

Findings delivered overnight. A rapid stakeholder session the following day produced a three-choice voting system that restored the collective premise, secured CEO approval, and resolved every critical failure before launch.

/Four launch-critical failures identified through compressed field validation
/Overnight diagnosis, next-day stakeholder reframe, CEO approval before opening
/Zero rework loop, no schedule slip

"Very new to a lot of Singaporeans, never seen before."

Fellow Singaporean during usability testing · June 2025
04 · The outcome

From one exhibition to a national civic engagement platform.

Dream Lab launched on schedule for the SG60 opening ceremony and now runs in three modes: a permanent installation at URA Centre supporting Draft Master Plan 2025, a modular roving exhibition across 14 locations over four months, and facilitated closed-door citizen workshops.
