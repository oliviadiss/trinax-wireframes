This is Trinax

Different specialists, one difficult problem.

Researchers, designers, engineers, producers and technicians sit on the same projects. Not in sequence, and not in separate buildings. The interaction designer hears the structural constraint the same week the engineer hears the visitor insight.

That is how ambitious ideas survive contact with reality. Someone has to know whether the idea can be built, powered, mounted, maintained and left running in a public space for three years. Here, that someone is in the room from the start.

You will see what you made being used. Then you will watch the parts of it that do not work, and fix them.

How we think

Evidence before build.

We would rather be wrong in a research session than wrong on opening day. Curiosity here means asking who this is for and what they will actually do, then testing it before anyone writes production code.

01 Double Diamond

Diverge, then decide. Twice. We open the problem up before narrowing it, then open the solution up before committing. It is uncomfortable, and it is why the second half of a project is calmer than most people expect.

02 Return on Experience

Every experience is measured against what it must achieve — dwell time, comprehension, footfall, recall, conversion. If we cannot say what changes for the person using it, we do not build it yet.

03 Challenge the assumption

Briefs arrive with answers already inside them. Part of the job is separating the requirement from the assumption, and saying so early — including when the assumption is ours.

04 Grow through exposure

You learn this work by seeing it. Field trips, site visits on live projects, overseas deployments, LUMA and human-centred design training, internal innovation initiatives, and continuous learning that is scheduled rather than hoped for.

What good looks like here

Eight behaviours, described honestly.

Not values on a wall. This is what people are actually recognised for.

Impact

Know what changes for the person using it.

If a feature cannot be tied to something a visitor does differently, argue it out or cut it.

Commitment

Own it through to opening day.

The person who scoped it is on site during install. Handover is not an exit.

Consistency

The 40th deployment matches the first.

Documented, tested, repeatable. Heroics on the night mean something was missed weeks earlier.

Initiative

Raise it before it becomes a problem.

Flag the risk, bring two options, and say which one you would pick.

Quality

Test it before the public does.

Quality assurance finishes in-house. Nothing goes live on the assumption that it probably works.

Collaboration

Sit with the discipline you are blocking.

Ten minutes next to the engineer beats four days of comments on a file.

Learning

Change your mind when the evidence does.

Being attached to your first idea is the expensive habit here.

Accountability

Say what went wrong, early, in the room.

Reviews name decisions, not people. The cost of a late admission lands on the whole team.

It may not be for you if

Read this part properly.

We would rather you decide now than six months in. None of this makes you a worse professional — it makes you a poor fit here.

You want a fixed remit. Roles here overlap by design. You will be asked to work outside the edges of your job title, often.
You wait to be told. Direction exists, but nobody will queue up work for you daily. The gap between briefs is yours to fill.
Feedback lands badly. Reviews here are direct and frequent. They are about the work, and they will not always be gentle.
Ambiguity unsettles you. Early-stage projects are genuinely unclear. Some of what you build will be discarded on the evidence.
High standards feel like pressure. Public installations do not get a soft launch. The bar is set by the person who walks up to it on day one.
